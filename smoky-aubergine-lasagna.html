Smoky Aubergine Lasagna

MainItalianServes 4

Ingredients

  • For the lasagne
  • 3 large aubergines
  • 4 garlic cloves, peeled and lightly crushed
  • 4 anchovy fillets
  • 4 tbsp olive oil
  • 400g cherry tomatoes
  • 1 red chilli, finely chopped
  • 2 x 400g tins chopped tomatoes
  • 1 small bunch basil, including the stalks
  • 1 tsp red wine vinegar
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • Sea salt and black pepper
  • Approximately 9–12 lasagne sheets
  • 75g Parmesan, finely grated
  • 100g low moisture mozzarella, grated
  • For the white sauce
  • 50g unsalted butter
  • 50g plain flour
  • 500ml whole milk
  • A good grating of nutmeg
  • Sea salt and black pepper
  • For the winter salad
  • ½ fennel bulb
  • 1 small radicchio or 2 little gem lettuces
  • 1 orange, zest and juice
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • 3 tbsp good extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • Honey to taste, if needed
  • Sea salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Char the aubergines directly over the flame of a gas hob, turning regularly, until the skins are blackened and the flesh has completely collapsed. Alternatively, place under a hot grill for around 10–15 minutes, turning occasionally. They need to be very soft and completely collapsed, and always take longer than you think.
  2. Transfer the aubergines to a bowl and cover with a plate or cling film. Leave to steam for 10–30 minutes while you make the tomato sauce. If using a broiler, place them directly under it for at least 10 minutes on each side.
  3. For the tomato sauce, add the olive oil, crushed garlic, basil (including stalks), anchovies and chopped chilli to a medium saucepan. Fry gently over a medium heat for 1–2 minutes until the garlic is starting to turn lightly golden and the anchovies have melted into the oil.
  4. Add the cherry tomatoes and cook for a few minutes, then pour in 100ml water. Bring to a simmer and cook for 5–10 minutes until the tomatoes start to burst and soften.
  5. Tip in the tinned tomatoes and let it bubble gently on a medium-low heat for 30 minutes, stirring occasionally, until reduced and thick and jammy. Crush any whole tomatoes with tongs or the back of a spoon as they cook.
  6. Stir in the vinegar and sugar, then taste and season with salt and pepper. Remove the basil stalks and set aside.
  7. For the white sauce, melt the butter in a saucepan over a medium heat, then stir in the flour. Cook for 2 minutes, stirring continuously.
  8. Gradually add the milk a little at a time, whisking until smooth between each addition. Cook for a few minutes until thickened but still loose enough to spread. Add a good grating of nutmeg and season with salt and pepper.
  9. Once the aubergines are cool enough to handle, peel away and discard the charred skins without washing them. Cut off the tops, then use the back of a fork to gently flatten the flesh into thick, roughly even layers, keeping the aubergines as intact as possible.
  10. Bring a large saucepan of salted water to the boil and blanch the lasagne sheets for 3–4 minutes until softened but not completely cooked through. Drain and lay them out separately on a clean tea towel to stop them sticking.
  11. Preheat the oven to 200°C/180°C fan.
  12. Spoon a thin layer of tomato sauce into the base of a baking dish, approximately 30 x 20cm. Add a layer of lasagne sheets, then more tomato sauce, a layer of aubergine, a little white sauce, a generous scattering of Parmesan and a topping of grated mozzarella.
  13. Repeat the layers until the dish is full, finishing with lasagne sheets, tomato sauce, white sauce and cheese.
  14. Bake covered with foil for around 30 minutes, then uncovered for a final 15–20 minutes, until bubbling with deeply golden, crisp edges. Leave to rest for 10 minutes before serving.
  15. For the salad, zest and juice the orange into a large bowl. Add the Dijon mustard, olive oil, red wine vinegar and a little salt and pepper, then whisk together.
  16. Finely slice the fennel and cut the radicchio or little gem into large pieces, separating the leaves. Toss in the dressing just before serving alongside the lasagne.
Notes
Use a small baking dish, approx. 30 x 20cm. Grilling or broiling the aubergines gives the same tender texture with less charred flavour (the author grills for her kids). Charred aubergine also works well in a traditional meat lasagne. Don't wash the aubergines after peeling, to keep the smoky flavour. Cook times: aubergine 10–15 min, sauce about 45 min, baking 45–50 min.
